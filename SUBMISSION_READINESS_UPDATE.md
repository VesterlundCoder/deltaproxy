**Date:** July 2026

## Executive conclusion

The theory/code package has been substantially corrected, but the paper is **not
yet submission-ready**. The remaining blockers are no longer mainly source-code
edits. They are corrected LUMI/RNS reruns, archival deposition of the resulting
logs/manifests, and independent mathematical review of the conditional theorem.

The most important audit finding was a product-order mismatch:

- the exact engine evaluated `P_N = M_1 M_2 ... M_N`;
- the old QR proxy evaluated the reversed product `M_N ... M_2 M_1`.

The corrected QR implementation now propagates `M_n.T @ Q`, which analyzes
`P_N.T` and therefore the same singular spectrum as the exact right product.
Consequently, all historical GPU accuracy, survivor-rate, enrichment and
speedup claims must be rerun or explicitly retained only as legacy engineering
motivation.

## 1.1 Product order corrected throughout

### Mathematical convention

\[
P_N=M_1M_2\cdots M_N.
\]

### Code changes

- `cmf_generic.py` keeps the exact update `P = P @ M_n`.
- `spectral_delta.py` now defaults to `product_order="right"` and applies
  `M_n.T @ Q`.
- `proxy_batch.py` applies transposed batched matrices.
- `gpu_proxy/proxy_kernel.py` applies the transpose companion action in the GPU
  kernel.
- `gpu_proxy/run_torch` and NumPy reference paths use the same convention.

### Tests

`tests/test_product_order.py` verifies:

1. incremental QR equals direct QR of `P_N.T` at small depth;
2. floating-point and pure-integer right products agree exactly at small depth.

**Consequence:** historical sweep outputs generated by the former kernel are not
submission evidence until rerun.

## 1.2 Observable definition made explicit

The former exact engine silently maximized over every ordered row pair. The
revised API now distinguishes:

### Fixed observable

```python
independent_delta(..., row_pair=(i, j))
```

### Fixed finite observable family

\[
\mathcal O=\{(e_i^T,e_j^T):i\ne j\},
\qquad
\delta_N^{\max}=\max_{\omega\in\mathcal O}\delta_N(\omega).
\]

```python
independent_delta(..., row_pair=None, return_pair=True)
```

returns both `delta_O^max` and the maximizing ordered pair.

New functions:

- `pair_deltas_int(...)`
- `stable_max_pair(...)`

The paper now includes a finite-family reduction proposition: if every member
converges uniformly, the finite maximum converges to the maximum of the limiting
deltas; a unique separated maximizer implies eventual pair stability.

**Remaining theoretical caution:** most numerical screening compares `r_2` with
`delta_O^max`, not with one fixed observable. This is an empirical screening
comparison unless pair stability is proved or demonstrated.

## 1.3 Visible-mode theorem corrected

The following defects were corrected in `lyapunov_delta_proxy.tex`:

### Dominance inequality

Correct form:

\[
\lambda_j\le \lambda_{J^\star}-\eta
\]

for every other visible error mode `j`.

### Denominator domination

The paper now explicitly assumes

\[
R_n=
\sum_{j\ne a}
\frac{A_j(n)\psi(u_j(n))}
     {A_a(n)\psi(u_a(n))}
\longrightarrow0.
\]

The denominator mode is not defined merely as the first pointwise nonzero
projection.

### Double-depth separation

The theorem explicitly assumes

\[
\frac{r_{2n}-L}{r_n-L}\to0.
\]

### Gauge dependence

The paper distinguishes projective invariance from arithmetic normalization and
states that the reported denominator sequence is an integer arithmetic-height
scale, not necessarily the denominator of the reduced rational number.

## 1.4 Exterior-product exponent corrected

The old text omitted one dominant denominator contribution. The corrected scale
is

\[
\log |(\varphi\wedge\psi)(v_n\wedge v_{2n})|
 =n(2\lambda_a+\lambda_{J^\star})+o(n),
\]

while

\[
\log|q_nq_{2n}|=3n\lambda_a+o(n).
\]

Therefore

\[
\log|r_n-r_{2n}|
 =n(\lambda_{J^\star}-\lambda_a)+o(n),
\]

which yields the stated delta ratio.

## 1.5 Degeneracy criterion corrected

The old filter rejected every trajectory with `shift[i] == -1`, even if the
root moved away from zero, and failed to state other positive-time zero
crossings.

The corrected finite-depth criterion checks

\[
f_i(n)=s_i+n d_i+1=0
\]

for an integer step in the inspected range.

New functions:

- `numerator_zero_steps(...)`
- `is_degenerate(..., n_start, n_end)`

The manuscript now calls this a **finite-depth nonsingularity** criterion. It is
not claimed to prove full effective dimension.

**Consequence:** all historical “full-dimensional” classifications and filtered
hit rates must be recomputed.

## 1.6 6F5 model made self-contained

The paper now explicitly defines

\[
f_i(n)=s_i+nd_i+1,
\qquad
g_j(n)=s_{6+j}+nd_{6+j}+2,
\]

\[
c_0(n)=-p e_6(f(n)),
\qquad
c_k(n)=q e_{6-k}(g(n))-p e_{6-k}(f(n)),
\]

and writes the complete `6 x 6` companion matrix and right-product convention.
A reader can now reconstruct the experimental object without reading Python.
